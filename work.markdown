# What I have built

Two of these are directly relevant to the assessment work, so I've described them
in some detail. The rest is listed briefly.

I co-led each of the projects below with my long-time partner Venky, who still works
with me when an engagement needs a delivery team rather than an assessment. Where
something was his rather than mine, it isn't on this page.

## TransIn — logistics, India

*[transin.in](https://www.transin.in/) · multi-tenant SaaS · event-driven
microservices*

TransIn moves cargo across the Indian port ecosystem. When we started, operations
ran on paper.

The interesting constraint was not technical. Each location ran its own operating
procedures, so there was no single process to automate — the system needed different
interfaces for different sites while still producing one coherent view of the
business. The management team wanted something they described as a lego kit, so they
could change strategy without rebuilding.

We took the operation from paper to fully digital in 15 months, supporting around
$10M in transactions. The team started at two people and grew to twenty.

What came out of it mattered more than the migration: a view of unutilised trips
that the business development team could actually sell against, predictability on
cash flows, vehicle tracking, and enough accumulated operating data that fleet
owners could access cheaper financing on the strength of it.

**Why this is the relevant one.** A multi-site organization with non-uniform
procedures and a reporting obligation spanning all of it is the same shape as a
nonprofit running several programs against several funders. The hard part isn't the
software. It's producing one trustworthy number out of sites that each do things
differently, without forcing them all to work the same way.

## Echojoy — payments and loyalty, India

*[echojoy.in](https://www.echojoy.in/) · gift cards across multiple brands ·
event-driven serverless*

Echojoy issues gift cards redeemable across many brands rather than one. I was part
of the founding team; we designed the architecture and built it out with four
developers over 18 months — web, iOS and Android.

It handles roughly $8M of gift cards annually and around $10M in transactions, and
the entire stack is run by a team of three.

**Why that last sentence is the point.** The system doesn't need people to operate
it. That's the distinction I care about in assessment work: a clean result produced
by a system, versus the same clean result produced by staff absorbing the work
every cycle. Both look identical from outside — until the people leave.

**Board** — independent director and audit committee chair of a BSE-listed company.

does comes down to a single question put to management: how do you know that number
is right?
